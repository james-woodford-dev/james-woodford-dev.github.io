Research

An overview of my research interests, ongoing projects, and the questions that motivate my work.

Overview

Making high-fidelity electrochemical simulation scale

Electrochemical energy devices such as fuel cells, electrolyzers, and hydrogen-carrier reactors depend on what happens inside porous materials: reactions at catalyst interfaces, coupled to charge, gas, and liquid transport through tortuous pore networks. Tomography can now image these microstructures in 3D at high resolution, but the numerical tools have not kept up. Pore-resolved models produce large, stiff, strongly nonlinear systems, and in practice modelers make large concessions to fit their computational budget: smaller domains, homogenized physics, simplified kinetics.

My Ph.D. focuses on removing those concessions. I work on finite element discretizations and solver strategies that scale to the domain sizes and physical detail that modern imaging provides. I pair that with hands-on experience in PFIB-SEM tomography and electrochemical modeling, so the methods are built around real materials and real questions.

Research Themes

From image to physics to solver

Scalable Finite Element Solvers

Discretization, preconditioning, and nonlinear solution strategies for coupled electrochemical transport problems, designed for distributed-memory clusters and GPUs, so that pore-resolved models can run at the scale of real electrode microstructures.

Keywords: finite element method, preconditioning, Newton–Krylov solvers, MPI, GPU computing

Electrochemical Interfaces & Transport

Microscale models that couple interfacial reaction kinetics, from Butler–Volmer to microkinetic descriptions, with charge transport and multiphase mass transport in catalyst layers and porous transport layers.

Keywords: catalyst layers, microkinetic modeling, two-phase transport, PEM fuel cells, water electrolysis

Image-Based Microstructure Modeling

Turning PFIB-SEM tomography into simulation-ready geometry, covering acquisition, segmentation, microstructural analysis, and meshing, so that models are grounded in the materials that are actually made.

Keywords: PFIB-SEM, FIB-SEM tomography, 3D reconstruction, image processing, porous media

Research Software Engineering

Open, tested, reproducible simulation software. I contribute to the open-source OpenFCST project and build automated pipelines that take a microstructure from image to simulation result on local and cluster resources.

Keywords: C++, Python, continuous integration, HPC workflows, OpenFCST

Projects

Current and past work

Active

Scalable solvers for pore-resolved electrochemical simulation

My Ph.D. research (2026–). The first step is identifying where current finite element pipelines break down as image-based domains grow, whether in assembly, linear solves, nonlinear convergence, or memory, and then developing methods that remove those bottlenecks without simplifying the physics.

Active

Microkinetic and pore-scale modeling of liquid organic hydrogen carriers

Coupling detailed surface chemistry with transport in porous catalysts for hydrogen storage in liquid organic carriers. This is a demanding test case for the solver work, because stiff microkinetic networks must be resolved alongside pore-scale transport.

Funding: Mitacs Accelerate

Completed

Image-based modeling of PEM fuel cell porous layers

M.Sc. research (2022–2026). I built a workflow to characterize porous carbon media with FIB-SEM tomography and extended OpenFCST's microscale electrochemical FEM solvers. I also developed an automation framework linking image processing, microstructural analysis, and transport simulation. Applications included the effect of PTFE coverage on water content in gas diffusion layers and of ionomer coverage on water intrusion and performance in catalyst layers.

Completed

Supported iridium oxide catalysts for oxygen evolution

Undergraduate research (2022, Semagina Lab). Synthesized metal-oxide-supported Ir catalysts by incipient wetness, tested them on a rotating disk electrode, and characterized them with SEM-EDX and XRD to relate support conductivity to oxygen evolution activity.

Funding: NSERC Undergraduate Student Research Award
